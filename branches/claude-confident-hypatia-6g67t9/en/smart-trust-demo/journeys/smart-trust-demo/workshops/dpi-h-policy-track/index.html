    Verifiable immunization records across borders: the policy-maker track

    The seven modules (M1 to M7) of the DPI-H workshop's policy-maker track, taught from the journeys J1 to J4.1 of one cross-border family: domestic and cross-border verifiable immunization records as the entry point to a personal health record. Converted from the VaxLink walkthrough's policyData.ts (scratch proof).

    For: Ministry of Health policy makers and digital health leads, at tables of one country each

    You will not leave with an assessment or a plan. You will leave knowing what each consists of, where your country stands on its first page, and who you would do the rest with.

    A child vaccinated in ___ should be able to ___ in ___.

    “Absent” is a finding, not a failure — the question is only whether it blocks this use case.

    Closing discussion

    1. M2 Identity for babies born abroad J1 J2b

      How should national immunization systems handle temporary IDs for cross-border infants before civil registration is complete?

    2. M4 Where trust begins J2a J3a

      Why should offline CHW devices store foreign cards as “reported, not verified” rather than trying to verify them in the field?

    3. M3 Matching vaccines across countries J3b

      When a monovalent vaccine is given abroad but the home schedule uses a combination (e.g. pentavalent), how should schedule rules calculate the doses still due?

    4. M5 Cross-border access and audit J4.0b J4.1

      What agreements do countries need so that a clinician viewing a record abroad triggers an audit entry back in the patient’s home country?
