    A Lao baby is born in Thailand

    Somsack gets his birth vaccines in a Thai hospital — and the only thing that will cross the border with him is a pair of paper cards.

    Without this

    The baby has no Thai ID number, so his records would normally stay locked inside the Thai hospital’s system.

    What makes it work

    A digitally signed QR card travels with the family — no ID number or database lookup needed.

    Ask the room

    What is the minimum a family should leave hospital with when their baby has no national ID?

    What if…

    The cards are lost on the way home.

    The doses still happened, but no proof travels. Lao clinicians must re-vaccinate or rely on memory.

    Policy takeaway

    Babies born abroad can get a verifiable vaccine record before they have the host country’s civil registration or a national ID — and registering the birth at home still matters.

    Policy questions for the table

    1. M1Write your country’s use-case statement: “A child vaccinated in ___ should be able to ___ in ___.”
    2. M4Who in your government would sign this card on behalf of the health sector: the hospital, the ministry, or a national trust service?
    3. M5Does your law let a facility issue a health credential to a child who is not a citizen and has no national ID?
