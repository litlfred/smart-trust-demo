    Every moment’s policy takeaway

    • J1
      A Lao baby is born in Thailand

      Babies born abroad can get a verifiable vaccine record before they have the host country’s civil registration or a national ID — and registering the birth at home still matters.

    • J2a
      A health worker finds the baby — and reads his cards

      Keep a clear line between what families report and what has been verified, and don’t grant trust too early.

    • J2b
      Registered at home, and referred

      Newborns can be registered at home, offline, before civil registration or a national ID exists.

    • J3a
      The clinic confirms the cards are genuine

      Trust is decentralized: a Lao clinic verifies a Thai signature itself, without contacting Thailand.

    • J3b
      Thai vaccines are fitted to the Lao schedule

      Never overwrite another country’s record: keep the original coding alongside the national one.

    • J4.0a
      One phone holds the whole family’s records

      Design for shared devices (“One Family, One Phone”) so families with only one phone aren’t left out.

    • J4.0b
      Dad creates a link to share at the clinic

      Data sovereignty: the record stays in Lao PDR until the family authorizes someone to open it.

    • J4.1
      A Thai doctor sees the full history

      Continuity of care across borders: each country keeps its own records, with no international patient registry and no vendor lock-in. What is agreed regionally is the data model and the trust list, not a shared copy of the data.
