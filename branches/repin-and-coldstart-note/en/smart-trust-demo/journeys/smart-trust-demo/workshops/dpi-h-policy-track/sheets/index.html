    Verifiable immunization records across borders: the policy-maker track

    Cover

    The scope promise. You will not leave with an assessment or a plan. You will leave knowing what each consists of, where your country stands on its first page, and who you would do the rest with.

    Start narrow

    One population, one credential, one facility type.

    e.g. Malaysia’s Hajj Health Card
    Start shared

    Build the foundation once for several programmes, not once for immunization.

    e.g. Taifafeki’s registry shared across parts of government
    Start borrowed

    Adopt a regional or international artefact rather than authoring your own.

    e.g. The ICVP IG, the IPS, a SNOMED subset

    M5 Governance, decision rights and the legal basis

    Who holds each decision — and what legal basis lets the record cross a border and be shared for a child?

    1. J1 Does your law let a facility issue a health credential to a child who is not a citizen and has no national ID?
    2. J2b Who may create a health identity for a newborn at a home visit, and on whose consent?
    3. J4.0a Who may hold and share a child’s record: either parent, a guardian, the child from what age? What does your law say about dependants?
    4. J4.0b What legal basis lets a record held at home be opened by a clinician abroad, on the family’s consent?
    5. J4.0b Would one clause in your procurement — conformance to the national IG — be enough for your portal to issue this link?
    6. J4.0b Who sets how a shared link is protected — the passcode, how long it lasts, who may open it: a national rule, or the family’s choice?
    7. J4.1 What agreement makes every view abroad leave an entry in the home country’s log — and who reads it?
