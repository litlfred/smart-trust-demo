    J3a

    The clinic confirms the cards are genuine

    Nurse Chanthason scans the card, and her tablet checks Taifafeki’s digital signature against the WHO trust list — without contacting Taifafeki.

    T0 + 4 weeks · Lomaru District Health Center · Clinic tablet (verification) · told through CH Chanthason

    Nurse Chanthason opens the referral and scans the QRs. The EHR verifies each DVC offline against that copy (STT-VER-001…009, STT-KID-002), without contacting Taifafeki. Both verify, so it imports them as FHIR Immunizations (TrustDemoJ3HepB, TrustDemoJ3Bcg) with provenance (TrustDemoJ3Verification; STT-VER-007, STT-VER-008), replacing the reported doses of J2. If a DVC fails (a tampered card, say), the EHR names the step and the reason, the doses stay unverified, and the nurse decides by national catch-up policy.

    Before this step (1)
    The DID documents are already loaded

    Precondition: the DID documents are already loaded. Loading them is not a step of this visit. The clinic may be offline all day, so the EHR fetches nothing during it: its trust store holds the GDHCN DEV trust list as it was last refreshed, including XXR's DSC DID document (STT-TRU-001, STT-TRU-002, STT-TRU-005). A clinic with no connection of its own refreshes it about weekly, when the CHW visits the base clinic or district health office, where Country B retrieves the list the GDHCN trust anchor distributes. A DSC published after that refresh is not in the clinic's copy, so its DVC fails the trust-list check (STT-VER-006) until the next refresh.

    What if…

    • The card has been tampered with. The signature check fails, the doses stay unverified and the nurse follows the local catch-up policy.

    Policy point. Trust is decentralized: an Onesan clinic verifies a Taifafeki signature itself, without contacting Taifafeki.

    Under the hood

    The clinic checks Taifafeki’s signature against WHO’s trust list, stored locally. One check turns a foreign paper card into trusted clinical data, with no call to Taifafeki.

    Requirements exercised

    • STT-VER-001 Verification order
      Statement

      The verifier SHALL run the QR Code Generation section 11 sequence in order: prefix, Base45, inflate, COSE parse, header validation, trust lookup by kid, signature verification, EKU check, CWT decode and time checks, subclaim dispatch, business rules.

    • STT-VER-002 No payload access before the signature is verified
      Statement

      The API SHALL make the payload and claims reachable only through a successful verification result type. Code that inspects an unverified payload SHALL go through an explicitly named unsafe or diagnostic call.

    • STT-VER-003 Defensive protected-header parsing
      Statement

      Before the signature is checked, the verifier SHALL validate the types and lengths in the protected header: alg is a supported integer, kid is a byte string of the expected length, and no duplicate labels. It SHALL fail closed on anything unexpected.

    • STT-VER-004 EKU check against certificate type
      Statement

      If the matching DSC has an Extended Key Usage extension with any of 1.3.6.1.4.1.1847.2021.1.1 (test), .1.2 (vaccinations) or .1.3 (recovery), the verifier SHALL check it against the certificate type of each hcert entry: DVC (-6) and DDCC VS (3) are vaccinations, DDCC TR (4) is test, and a DCC (1) is the type of its single v, t or r entry. An entry whose type the EKU does not permit SHALL be returned marked as not authorised (keyUsageAuthorised false), with a diagnostic; verification SHALL fail with KeyUsageMismatch only when no entry is authorised. A DSC with no such extension SHALL be accepted for any type.

    • STT-VER-005 Time checks with an injected clock
      Statement

      The verifier SHALL reject a certificate whose exp is in the past, and one whose iat is in the future beyond a configurable clock-skew tolerance. The current time SHALL be passed in by the caller (now), never read from an ambient clock.

    • STT-VER-006 Trust-list presence is the DSC validity check
      Statement

      The verifier SHALL NOT need CRL or OCSP access. A DSC is valid if it is on the current trust list. The verifier SHOULD also check the DSC's X.509 validity period against the injected clock.

    • STT-VER-007 Dispatch to logical model, then business rules
      Statement

      After verification the verifier SHALL decode each hcert entry with the codec registered for its subclaim, falling back to RawPayload. It SHALL then run any registered BusinessRuleHooks and attach their outcomes to the result.

    • STT-VER-008 Typed verification result and failure taxonomy
      Statement

      A certificate failing verification SHALL produce a Failed result, not an exception or panic. Failed results SHALL name the step that failed and a reason from a closed set shared by both languages: BadPrefix, UnsupportedContextVersion, Base45Invalid, InflateFailed, LimitExceeded, CoseMalformed, HeaderInvalid, UnsupportedAlgorithm, KidNotFound, SignatureInvalid, KeyUsageMismatch, Expired, NotYetValid, HcertMissing, MultipleSubclaims, PayloadInvalid. Exceptions or errors SHALL be reserved for programming errors and infrastructure failures.

    • STT-VER-009 Fully offline verification
      Statement

      Given a cached or static trust list, verification SHALL need no network access.

    • STT-KID-002 kid is a lookup hint, not unique
      Statement

      Trust-list lookup by kid SHALL return every matching DSC, and the verifier SHALL try each candidate until one verifies. A kid match alone SHALL NOT count as authentication.

    J3b

    Taifafeki vaccines are fitted to the Onesan schedule

    The Taifafeki Hep B product doesn’t match anything on the Onesan schedule. Chanthason makes the clinical call, and the system works out what’s due next.

    T0 + 4 weeks · Lomaru District Health Center · Clinic tablet (vaccine matching + schedule) · told through CH Chanthason

    The Taifafeki Hep B product matches nothing on the national schedule. The EHR keeps Taifafeki's product coding and adds the schedule coding beside it, from the national map (TrustDemoProductToOnesaEpi). For the Hep B product the map has no entry, so Nurse Chanthason makes the clinical call that it is the schedule's birth dose (TrustDemoJ3Adjudication), and her decision is added to the map for the next clinic. The schedule (TrustDemoOnesaInfantSchedule) then runs: the birth doses count, and pentavalent 1, bOPV 1 and PCV 1 are due at 6 weeks (TrustDemoJ3Recommendation). Khamla leaves with the date (TrustDemoJ3Reminder). Over the year Somsak gets those doses and measles-rubella at 9 months, in Onesa.

    What if…

    • There is no automatic match for the foreign vaccine. That is exactly this step. The nurse decides — and her decision could be shared so the next nurse doesn’t have to.

    Policy point. Never overwrite another country’s record: keep the original coding alongside the national one.

    Under the hood

    Shared WHO codes and a FHIR ConceptMap translate Taifafeki vaccines into the Onesan schedule. Computable schedule logic then works out what’s due next.

    Building blocks used

    Data standards
    • WHO SMART ImmunizationsstandardInternationalCommon PreQual codes make the mapping possible
      What it is

      WHO’s machine-readable immunization guidance: data dictionary, FHIR profiles, PreQual vaccine product codes and schedule logic.

      So what: Both countries use the same WHO vaccine codes and profiles, so a dose recorded in Taifafeki means the same thing in Onesa.

      WHO SMART Immunizations

    • ConceptMapstandardInternationalPreQual product → WHO vaccine type → Onesan EPI code
      What it is

      A FHIR resource that records how a code in one system maps to a code in another, including who decided and how confident they are.

      So what: Foreign vaccines are translated without overwriting them. The original Taifafeki code stays alongside the Onesan one.

      FHIR ConceptMap · The map in this journey (IG)

    • PlanDefinition / CQLstandardInternationalOnesan schedule logic calculates the 6-week doses
      What it is

      Computable rules for the national schedule that work out which doses are due next.

      So what: Once foreign doses are mapped, they automatically count toward the Onesan schedule. No mental arithmetic for the nurse.
