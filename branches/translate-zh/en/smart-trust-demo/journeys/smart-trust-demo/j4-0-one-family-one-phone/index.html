    One phone holds the whole family’s records

    A year on, the family plans a trip to Taifafeki. Bounmy’s phone holds records for all three of them, including Somsak’s six verified doses.

    T0 + 12 months · Lomaru, Onesa · Family wallet app on Dad’s phone · told through BS Bounmy

    The family plans a trip to Taifafeki. Bounmy's phone holds the family wallet, with a profile for each of them. Somsak's summary (TrustDemoJ4Ips) shows six doses: two from Taifafeki, four from Onesa.

    What if…

    • Khamla travels alone with Somsak. Who can access the baby’s record without Bounmy’s phone? Delegated access within a household is still an open design question.

    Policy point. Design for shared devices (“One Family, One Phone”) so families with only one phone aren’t left out.

    Under the hood

    A year of records from two countries is assembled into one International Patient Summary, a format any clinician abroad knows how to read.

      What it is

      HL7 FHIR

    • Info Gateway (AuthZ)toolInternationalLets Bounmy’s wallet read only his own household’s records
      What it is

      The authorization (AuthZ) layer in front of the national FHIR server. It checks the caller’s access token from the identity provider, applies access rules (role, facility, household, consent) to every request, and records each access.

      So what: Every app, from the CHW phone to the Taifafeki clinic viewer, goes through one front door. Onesa keeps the records at home and decides who can read or write what, including requests from abroad.

    J4.0b

    Dad creates a link to share at the clinic

    Before the trip, Bounmy creates a QR link for Somsak’s record, locked with a 4-digit passcode and valid for 14 days (the demo’s settings: each country would set its own). The record itself stays in Onesa.

    T0 + 12 months · Onesan–Taifafeki Friendship Bridge · Family wallet app (share link) · told through BS Bounmy

    For Somsak, Bounmy shares his record with a 4-digit passcode he can say aloud, valid for 14 days. The portal records his consent (TrustDemoJ4Consent), then does three things:

    1. It assembles the IPS and Somsak's DVCs.
    2. It encrypts them (STT-VHL-004, STT-VHL-007) behind a passcode-protected manifest with an expiry (STT-VHL-009).
    3. It issues the link as an HCERT, with the VHL at key 5 (STT-VHL-001, STT-VHL-002), signed with Country B's DSC.

    The QR holds only a pointer and a key: the record stays in Onesa.

    Before this step (1)
    Country B's DSC is shared with the trust anchor

    Precondition, as in J1: Country B's DSC, under Country B's CSCA, is already shared with the GDHCN, the Trust Anchor. That is what lets the clinic in Taifafeki verify the link in J4.1.

    What if…

    • Bounmy’s phone battery dies at the clinic. The link could be printed in advance, and the original paper cards still prove the birth doses.

    Policy point. Data sovereignty: the record stays in Onesa until the family authorizes someone to open it.

    Under the hood

    The QR is a key, not a copy. The record stays on the Onesan server, and the family’s consent (who, how long) is recorded in a standard form and enforced.

    Requirements exercised

    • STT-VHL-004 VHL key generation
      Statement

      The library SHALL generate a VHL key as 32 bytes from the platform's cryptographically secure random generator, encoded as 43 base64url characters.

    • STT-VHL-007 Document encryption: JWE dir / A256GCM
      Statement

      Document binaries SHALL be encrypted and decrypted as JWE Compact Serialization (RFC 7516) with alg dir and enc A256GCM (RFC 7518), the VHL key as the content key and the protected header as additional authenticated data, following SMART Health Links. Decryption SHALL refuse any other alg or enc, SHALL accept zip DEF, and SHALL fail on a bad tag. Encryption SHALL set cty to the document's content type.

    • STT-VHL-009 VHL expiry
      Statement

      Validating a VHL payload against a caller-supplied now SHALL report an expired link when the payload's exp has passed, in addition to the HCERT exp check made by the verifier.

    • STT-VHL-001 VHL payload in the SMART Health Links payload format
      Statement

      The library SHALL model the VHL payload as the SMART Health Links payload format that IHE VHL reuses: url (required, https), key (required, 43 base64url characters encoding 32 bytes), exp (optional integer, epoch seconds), flag (optional; the letters L and P in alphabetical order), label (optional, at most 80 characters), v (optional integer, default 1) and extension.fhirBaseUrl (optional). Validation SHALL report each violation. The single-file U flag is out of scope and SHALL be reported as unsupported.

    • STT-VHL-002 VHL payload at HCERT key 5 as a CBOR map
      Statement

      The issuer SHALL carry the VHL payload at HCERT claim -260 key 5 as a CBOR map with text keys, exp and v as CBOR integers. The verifier SHALL read key 5 as that map, and SHALL also accept a vhlink:/ text string there, so that VHLs built to IHE ITI-YY3 still verify.
