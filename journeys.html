    One family, two countries, and a trip:

    • Country A (birth country) is XXR, a GDHCN DEV participant. Chanthala is born there while her mother visits relatives, and gets her birth doses there (J1).
    • Country B (country of residence, “home”) is a fictional GDHCN DEV participant, written XXH. The family lives there (J2, J3, J4.0).
    • J4.1 takes place in a third country the family travels to.

    The family are Lao nationals. Nationality LAO is a field of the signed certificates, not a country in the journeys. Every person is fictional. Chanthala, Mali and Dr Pimchanok WATTANA come from the WHO guide’s QR Code Generation §12 example, whose two signed DVCs (Hep B, BCG) verify against the live GDHCN DEV trust list.

    smart-trust-phw’s requirements map onto the journeys as follows:

    • provide-a-vhl-to-a-receiver (Holder) is J2, J3 and J4.1.
    • receive-a-vhl is J2, J3 and J4.1 too. It names the Issuer as its actor, which looks wrong: OI-01.
    • publish-, receive-, distribute- and retrieve-pki-material are the trust anchor and the participants in J3.

    User scenarios

    Each step names the requirements of smart-trust-tools it exercises. The stories are in scenarios/stories.json.

    J1 — Cross-border birth and initial vaccination (Country A)

    Precondition: the issuer’s DSC is on the trust list. XXR’s document signer certificate (DSC), issued under XXR’s Country Signing CA (CSCA), was shared with the GDHCN, the Trust Anchor, before any DVC is signed; the GDHCN publishes it as a DID document. Sharing it is not a step of issuing. A DVC signed with a key whose DSC was never shared fails every receiver’s trust-list check (STT-VER-006).

    Mali gives birth to Chanthala on 2026-10-21 at a provincial hospital in Country A, while visiting relatives. Dr Pimchanok WATTANA gives the Hep B and BCG birth doses and records them. For each dose, the hospital system does three things:

    1. It builds a DVCMinPreQual payload: STT-PAY-001…005 and STT-PAY-007, with subclaim −6 (STT-SEL-003).
    2. It prepares the CWT and the bytes to sign: STT-CWT-001…005, STT-ISS-004.
    3. It has XXR’s key sign them (STT-COS-002, STT-COS-006), then finishes the HC1 text: STT-COS-001, STT-ZIP-001, STT-B45-001, STT-HC1-001, STT-QR-001.

    Mali leaves with two paper DVCs. In the demo: the guide’s two signed HC1 strings stand in for the hospital’s output. Live issuance in the app uses a TEST-ONLY key (STT-ISS-003), whose DSC is not on the GDHCN list, so its DVCs do not verify in J3.

    J2 — Return home, CHW discovery and referral (Country B)

    Back in Country B, Sita RAI finds Chanthala missing from the household register on a home visit. Sita has no signal. She scans both QRs. The CHW app reads them with read-unverified (STT-VER-011; STT-B45-002, STT-ZIP-002, STT-CBR-003), which needs no trust list. It shows the doses, marked unverified. Sita registers Chanthala and refers her to the clinic. Everything syncs that evening.

    J3 — Clinic verification and schedule generation (Country B)

    Precondition: the DID documents are already loaded. Loading them is not a step of this visit. The clinic may be offline all day, so the EHR fetches nothing during it: its trust store holds the GDHCN DEV trust list as it was last refreshed, including XXR’s DSC DID document (STT-TRU-001, STT-TRU-002, STT-TRU-005). A clinic with no connection of its own refreshes it about weekly, when the CHW visits the base clinic or district health office, where Country B retrieves the list the GDHCN trust anchor distributes. A DSC published after that refresh is not in the clinic’s copy, so its DVC fails the trust-list check (STT-VER-006) until the next refresh.

    Tomas ALVES opens the referral and scans the QRs. The EHR verifies each DVC offline against that copy (STT-VER-001…009, STT-KID-002). Both verify, so it imports them as FHIR Immunizations with provenance (STT-VER-007, STT-VER-008). The national schedule then runs: the birth doses count, and birth-dose OPV is due. Tomas gives it. If a DVC fails, the EHR names the step and the reason, and Tomas decides by national policy.

    J4.0 — “One Family, One Phone” (Country B)

    Precondition, as in J1: Country B’s DSC, under Country B’s CSCA, is already shared with the GDHCN, the Trust Anchor. That is what lets the hospital abroad verify the link in J4.1.

    The family plans a trip. On the national portal, Mali asks for each member’s records. For each member, the portal does three things:

    1. It assembles an IPS and that member’s DVCs.
    2. It encrypts them (STT-VHL-004, STT-VHL-007) behind a passcode-protected manifest with an expiry (STT-VHL-009).
    3. It issues the link as an HCERT, with the VHL at key 5 (STT-VHL-001, STT-VHL-002), signed with Country B’s DSC.

    The shared phone holds one QR per member.

    J4.1 — Point of care abroad

    Precondition, as in J3: the DID documents are already loaded. The hospital system’s trust store is a cached copy of the GDHCN trust list, including Country B’s DSC DID document (STT-TRU-001, STT-TRU-002, STT-TRU-005). The hospital refreshes it nightly or hourly, against about weekly at the offline J3 clinic. It does not need to be online to verify: verifying reads the cache and never fetches the list. A DSC published after the last refresh fails the trust-list check (STT-VER-006), naming that reason.

    Abroad, Chanthala has a fever. Mali shows Chanthala’s QR on the family phone and tells Dr Ana MOREIRA the passcode. The hospital system takes four steps:

    1. It verifies the HCERT against Country B’s DSC in that copy of the trust list.
    2. It requests the manifest by ITI-YY5, signed with HTTP Message Signatures (STT-VHL-005, STT-VHL-006, STT-VHL-008).
    3. It decrypts the IPS and DVCs (STT-VHL-007).
    4. It shows them to Dr Ana MOREIRA.

    If the HCERT does not verify (for example, Country B’s DSC is missing from the cached list), there is no digital verification. The hospital names the step and the reason (STT-VER-008), does not follow the VHL, and Dr Ana MOREIRA treats whatever the family can show as she would a paper record.
