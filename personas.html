    These are the generic personas of the journeys J1 to J4: the roles that play the lanes of the business processes, each defined as an ActorDefinition. The people and systems that play them in the journeys are fictional.

    Generic personas

    Role Description Played by (fictional) Also known as
    Caregiver The parent who holds the child’s paper DVCs and the family phone, and presents them; IHE ITI VHL VHL Holder and smart-trust Holder. Bounmy SENGMANY (father, holds the family phone); Khamla SENGMANY (mother) VHL Holder (IHE ITI VHL); Holder (SMART Trust)
    Subject of care The person the records are about: the child whose doses are given, signed, registered, verified and shared. Acts through the caregiver, holds no device and plays no lane in the journeys’ processes. Somsak SENGMANY (newborn) Patient; Care recipient
    Birth clinic vaccinator The clinician abroad who gives the birth doses and hands over the signed paper DVC (J1). Dr Anan (clinician, Kesaro, Taifafeki)  
    Community health worker Finds unregistered children on home visits, registers them and refers them to the clinic (J2). Vilay (community health worker, Village 04, Onesa)  
    Home clinic provider The nurse at the home clinic who receives the referral, has the foreign DVCs verified and decides catch-up doses (J3). Nurse Chanthason (Lomaru District Health Center, Onesa)  
    Point-of-care provider The clinician abroad who scans the family’s QR and reads the child’s records (J4.1). Dr Anan (clinician, Kesaro, Taifafeki)

    CHW app The OHS app on the CHW’s phone: reads a foreign DVC without verifying it (triage read, STT-VER-011), registers the child and records a referral, all offline, syncing later. CHW app, Onesa (Country B) Triage reader (SMART Trust Receiver, read-unverified)
    Home clinic EHR The clinic’s OHS-based record system: verifies DVCs against the GDHCN trust list, imports them as FHIR Immunization resources and evaluates the national schedule (J3). Lomaru District Health Center EHR (Country B) Verifier at the home clinic (SMART Trust Receiver)
    National health portal The home country’s portal that issues each family member’s records (IPS, DVCs) as a passcode-protected Verifiable Health Link and hosts the manifests (J4.0), checking the passcode, expiry and consent on each request and logging every access at home (J4.1): the VHL Sharer.     The people of the journeys

    Each is fictional. Their goals, needs and frustrations are kept in scenarios/actors/*.json (meta.persona), and the journeys viewer shows the same text.

    Dr. Anan (Taifafeki clinician · birth & follow-up, Taifafeki)

    Persona: a Taifafeki clinician in Kesaro who gives Somsak’s Hep B and BCG birth doses and prints the two signed DVCs (J1), and who sees him again a year later at a primary care clinic, reading his record through the VHL (J4.1). Fictional.

    • Goal: Give a non-Taifafeki baby proper care, at birth and when he returns.
    • Needs: A way to issue records that travel with the family; A child’s full history at the point of care; Confidence the data is authentic
    • Frustrations: No national ID to attach records to; Records from abroad he cannot read or verify

    Bounmy (Father · holds the family phone, Onesa)

    Persona: Somsak’s father, 29, head of the household in Onesa, who holds the family’s one shared phone (J4). Creates Somsak’s passcode-protected VHL (J4.0) and says the passcode aloud at the clinic (J4.1). Fictional.

    • Goal: Keep the whole family’s health records in one place he controls.
    • Needs: One app for three people on one phone; Share records abroad without paperwork; Decide who sees what, and for how long
    • Frustrations: Carrying folders of documents across the border; Not knowing who has seen his child’s data

    Chanthason (Nurse · District Health Center, Onesa)

    Persona for J3: the nurse at Lomaru District Health Center in Onesa (Country B), on the clinic’s tablet (OHS app, facility configuration). Makes the clinical call when a foreign product has no mapping. Fictional.

    • Goal: Trust foreign records enough to avoid repeating or missing vaccines.
    • Needs: A reliable yes/no on whether a card is genuine; Foreign vaccines translated to the Onesan schedule; Automatic calculation of what is due next
    • Frustrations: Unfamiliar product names from other countries; Choosing between re-vaccinating and trusting paper blindly

    Khamla (Mother, Onesa)

    Persona: Somsak’s mother, 26, an Onesan national living in Onesa (Country B) who gave birth in Taifafeki (Country A). Holds the two paper DVCs from J1 to J3. Fictional.

    • Goal: Have Somsak’s care recognized at home, without starting over.
    • Needs: Proof of the vaccines her baby got abroad; Clear guidance on when to come back; Not to be penalized for giving birth across the border
    • Frustrations: Paper cards are easy to lose; Extra trips to the clinic; The family’s records live on her husband’s phone

    Somsak (Baby · the record follows him, Onesa)

    The subject of every journey: an Onesan baby boy born 2025-10-28 at Kesaro Provincial Hospital, Taifafeki (Country A), while his family lives in Onesa (Country B). Has no Taifafeki identity number; registered in Onesa with a temporary id by the CHW (J2). Holds no device and acts through his parents. The story is the VaxLink workshop walkthrough’s (fredhersch/vaxlink). Fictional.

    • Goal: Get every vaccine he needs, once, wherever he is.
    • Needs: His birth doses to count at home; No repeated or missed vaccines; His history to be there when a doctor needs it
    • Frustrations: Born where he has no national ID; Invisible to the home system until someone registers him

    Vilay (Community health worker · Village 04, Onesa)

    Persona for J2: the community health worker of catchment Village 04 in Onesa (Country B). Works offline for weeks at a time on an Android phone (OHS app, community configuration) and syncs when she reaches the health center. Can register a child and refer, but cannot decide whether a foreign record is genuine. Fictional.

    • Goal: Make sure no newborn in her village is missed, even with no signal.
    • Needs: Tools that work offline for weeks; A fast way to capture foreign cards; A clear next step to hand the family
    • Frustrations: Can’t confirm whether a foreign card is genuine; Paperwork that duplicates what the clinic does
