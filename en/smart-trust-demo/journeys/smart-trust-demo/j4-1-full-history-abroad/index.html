    Step by step

    J4.1

    A Taifafeki doctor sees the full history

    Back where it all began, Dr. Anan scans the link, hears the passcode, and sees Somsak’s whole first year — and Onesa is told who looked.

    T0 + 12 months · Kesaro primary care clinic · Clinic viewer (scan link + passcode) · told through DA Dr. Anan

    Back where it began, Dr Anan sees Somsak at a primary care clinic in Kesaro. Bounmy shows the QR on his phone and says the passcode. The clinic viewer and the Onesan gateway take five steps:

    1. The viewer verifies the HCERT against Country B's DSC in its copy of the trust list.
    2. It requests the manifest by ITI-YY5, signed with HTTP Message Signatures (STT-VHL-005, STT-VHL-006, STT-VHL-008).
    3. The Onesan gateway checks the passcode, the expiry and the consent, returns the manifest, and logs the access at home (TrustDemoJ41Access).
    4. The viewer decrypts the IPS and DVCs (STT-VHL-007) (TrustDemoJ41Received).
    5. It shows them to Dr Anan: every dose since the birth, viewed, never copied.

    A wrong passcode is refused, and the attempt is still logged in Onesa (TrustDemoJ41WrongPasscode). If the HCERT does not verify (for example, Country B's DSC is missing from the cached list), there is no digital verification. The clinic names the step and the reason (STT-VER-008), does not follow the VHL, and Dr Anan treats whatever the family can show as he would a paper record.

    Before this step (1)
    The DID documents are already loaded

    Precondition, as in J3: the DID documents are already loaded. The clinic viewer's trust store is a cached copy of the GDHCN trust list, including Country B's DSC DID document (STT-TRU-001, STT-TRU-002, STT-TRU-005). The clinic refreshes it nightly or hourly, against about weekly at the offline J3 clinic. It does not need to be online to verify: verifying reads the cache and never fetches the list. A DSC published after the last refresh fails the trust-list check (STT-VER-006), naming that reason.

    What if…

    • The wrong passcode is entered. Access is refused and the attempt is still logged back in Onesa.

    Not yet specified

    • No agreement yet says who in Onesa reads the access log, or how often.

    Policy point. Continuity of care across borders: each country keeps its own records, with no international patient registry and no vendor lock-in. What is agreed regionally is the data model and the trust list, not a shared copy of the data.

    Under the hood

    A standard viewer fetches the record from Onesa, decrypts it in memory, and leaves an audit trail at home. Cross-border care with no shared database between the countries.

    Building blocks used

    Tools & apps
    • VHL viewersystemTaifafekiScans QR, takes passcode, displays IPS
      What it is

      A web or tablet app that scans a VHL QR, takes the passcode, fetches the record and displays it.

      So what: The Taifafeki clinic needs no account in the Onesan system. Any viewer that follows the VHL standard works.

      IHE ITI VHL (Receiver)

    • Info Gateway (AuthZ)toolInternationalAuthorizes the request against passcode and consent, releases data, writes the AuditEvent
      What it is

      The authorization (AuthZ) layer in front of the national FHIR server. It checks the caller’s access token from the identity provider, applies access rules (role, facility, household, consent) to every request, and records each access.

      So what: Every app, from the CHW phone to the Taifafeki clinic viewer, goes through one front door. Onesa keeps the records at home and decides who can read or write what, including requests from abroad.

      OHS FHIR Info Gateway

    • FHIR Data PipestoolInternationalFeeds AuditEvents into a Ministry dashboard of cross-border access, so unusual access stands out
      What it is

      Open-source pipelines that copy FHIR data from the national server, including Provenance and AuditEvent, into analytics-ready tables for reports and dashboards.

      So what: The Ministry sees patterns, not just single records: how many foreign certificates were verified, how often records are opened abroad, and any unusual access.

      OHS FHIR Data Pipes

    Trust & exchange
    • Verifiable Health LinkstandardInternationalManifest fetched; decryption key used
      What it is

      A signed QR that holds a link and decryption key, not the data itself. The record is fetched from its home only when the link is used.

      So what: Data stays in the home country until someone the family chooses opens it. The QR alone reveals nothing.

      IHE ITI Verifiable Health Links

    • Audit logstandardInternationalAuditEvent recorded in Onesa
      What it is

      A standard record of who accessed which data, when, and with what result.

      So what: Every cross-border view is logged at home, which supports accountability and family notification.

      IHE BALP · FHIR AuditEvent · The access log entry (IG)

    Data standards
    • IPSstandardInternationalRendered for the Taifafeki clinician
      What it is

      A standard, minimal health summary (immunizations, allergies, medications, problems) designed for care across borders.

      So what: The Taifafeki doctor gets a predictable summary they know how to read, not a dump of another country’s records.

    Requirements exercised

    • STT-VHL-005 ITI-YY5 manifest request
      Statement

      The library SHALL build the ITI-YY5 Retrieve Manifest request from a VHL payload: POST to <base>/List/_search (accepting a manifest URL of either the List?... or the List/_search?... form), Content-Type application/x-www-form-urlencoded, body holding the manifest URL's search parameters (_id, code, status and patient.identifier required; _include=List:item optional) plus recipient (required), passcode (required when, and only when, the payload's flag has P) and embeddedLengthMax (optional). It SHALL refuse a payload whose URL lacks a required parameter. Sending the request is the host's.

    • STT-VHL-006 ITI-YY5 manifest response
      Statement

      The library SHALL read the ITI-YY5 response, a FHIR searchset Bundle: the List with search.mode match and, when the Include DocumentReference option was used, the DocumentReferences with search.mode include. For each document it SHALL return the attachment URL and the decrypted content type (attachment.contentType); for List items whose DocumentReference is not included it SHALL return the reference for the host to read.

    • STT-VHL-008 Receiver authentication: HTTP Message Signatures
      Statement

      For the ITI-YY5 HTTP Message Signatures option the library SHALL compute Content-Digest (RFC 9530, sha-256, Structured Field byte sequence) and the RFC 9421 signature base over @method, @path, @authority, content-type and content-digest with created, keyid and alg parameters, return the bytes for the host to sign with the receiver's key, and assemble the Signature-Input and Signature headers from the result. For ecdsa-p256-sha256 the signature SHALL be the 64-byte r||s; a DER signature SHALL be converted.

    • STT-VHL-007 Document encryption: JWE dir / A256GCM
      Statement

      Document binaries SHALL be encrypted and decrypted as JWE Compact Serialization (RFC 7516) with alg dir and enc A256GCM (RFC 7518), the VHL key as the content key and the protected header as additional authenticated data, following SMART Health Links. Decryption SHALL refuse any other alg or enc, SHALL accept zip DEF, and SHALL fail on a bad tag. Encryption SHALL set cty to the document's content type.

    • STT-VER-008 Typed verification result and failure taxonomy
      Statement

      A certificate failing verification SHALL produce a Failed result, not an exception or panic. Failed results SHALL name the step that failed and a reason from a closed set shared by both languages: BadPrefix, UnsupportedContextVersion, Base45Invalid, InflateFailed, LimitExceeded, CoseMalformed, HeaderInvalid, UnsupportedAlgorithm, KidNotFound, SignatureInvalid, KeyUsageMismatch, Expired, NotYetValid, HcertMissing, MultipleSubclaims, PayloadInvalid. Exceptions or errors SHALL be reserved for programming errors and infrastructure failures.
