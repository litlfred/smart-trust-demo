    Architecture: two countries on one trust foundation

    Each country builds its own stack on the layers of the DPI-H reference architecture. Underneath both is WHO GDHCN: the shared trust list that lets an Onesan clinic believe a Taifafeki certificate, and a Taifafeki clinic believe an Onesan link, with no direct connection between the two.

    Foundation: GDHCN DEV trust network (WHO), WHO SMART Trust specifications (HCERT, DVC, VHL)
    • Taifafeki publishes the key it signs certificates with.
    • Onesa caches the list, so its clinics verify Taifafeki cards offline; Taifafeki does the same for Onesan VHLs.
    • Data crosses only as a signed QR the family carries, or a VHL the family shares: never system to system.

    Speak the same language

    FHIR and WHO SMART Guidelines mean a dose recorded in Taifafeki has the same structure and codes in Onesa.

    FHIR R4WHO SMART ImmunizationsConceptMapIPS
    Trust without phoning home

    Countries sign records and publish their keys on WHO GDHCN, so anyone can verify a certificate, even offline.

    Digital Vaccine CertificateWHO GDHCNProvenance
    Share without copying

    Data stays in its home country. The family hands out a time-limited key, and every access is logged at home.

    Verifiable Health LinkConsent (PCF)Info Gateway (AuthZ)Audit log
    Open tools, offline first

    Open Health Stack gives every app offline FHIR storage and configurable forms, one secure front door to the national server, and analytics on top.
