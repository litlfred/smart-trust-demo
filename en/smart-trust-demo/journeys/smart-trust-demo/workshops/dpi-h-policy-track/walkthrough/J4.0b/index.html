    Dad creates a link to share at the clinic

    Before the trip, Bounmy creates a QR link for Somsak’s record, locked with a 4-digit passcode and valid for 14 days (the demo’s settings: each country would set its own). The record itself stays in Onesa.

    Without this

    Sending photos or PDFs of records leaks data and can’t be verified by the receiver.

    What makes it work

    A Verifiable Health Link: the QR holds only a locked pointer; the data stays home until the passcode is given.

    Hand-off: A QR on Dad’s phone + a passcode he can say aloud

    Ask the room

    Is a 4-digit spoken passcode the right balance of security and ease for this family?

    What if…

    Bounmy’s phone battery dies at the clinic.

    The link could be printed in advance, and the original paper cards still prove the birth doses.

    Policy takeaway

    Data sovereignty: the record stays in Onesa until the family authorizes someone to open it.

    Policy questions for the table

    1. M5What legal basis lets a record held at home be opened by a clinician abroad, on the family’s consent?
    2. M5Would one clause in your procurement — conformance to the national IG — be enough for your portal to issue this link?
    3. M5Who sets how a shared link is protected — the passcode, how long it lasts, who may open it: a national rule, or the family’s choice?
    4. M6Should the part of the record that crosses borders (IPS plus an immunization core) be profiled once for the region, with national extensions left sovereign?
