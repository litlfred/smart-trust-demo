    Without this

    Many families share one smartphone; one-app-per-person designs leave them out.

    What makes it work

    A household wallet on one phone, with a separate health summary for each person.

    Ask the room

    How do we design for shared phones without compromising each person’s privacy?

    What if…

    Khamla travels alone with Somsak.

    Who can access the baby’s record without Bounmy’s phone? Delegated access within a household is still an open design question.

    Policy takeaway

    Design for shared devices (“One Family, One Phone”) so families with only one phone aren’t left out.

    Policy questions for the table

    1. M4Is a family wallet a national service, a private app, or both — and who decides which apps may hold a record?
    2. M5Who may hold and share a child’s record: either parent, a guardian, the child from what age? What does your law say about dependants?

    Start narrow ★— One credential first: the child’s vaccination record, on the phone the family already has.

    A wallet or citizen app that holds a family’s records
    Rules for a parent holding a child’s record

    “Absent” is a finding, not a failure — the question is only whether it blocks this use case.
